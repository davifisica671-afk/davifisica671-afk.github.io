Hello, world: we are building Refract in public

What Refract is, why we are building it in the open, and what you can expect from this blog: real decisions, real mistakes, real progress.

If you're reading this, the blog works. That felt like the right first post.

Refract is a native Windows companion that puts AI where you actually work: it sees what's on your screen when you allow it to, answers instantly, takes dictation with on-device speech recognition, and follows you into the browser with a companion extension. We're building the tool we want to use every day — and we decided to build it in public.

Why in public?

Because software built in the open gets better faster. Sharing what we ship — and what breaks along the way — keeps us honest, and it means every decision we make gets scrutinized by the people who actually use the thing. No marketing fog, no "coming soon" pages that never come. When something ships, you'll read about it here, including what went wrong.

What to expect

  • Engineering deep dives — how the pieces actually work: the desktop app, the browser extension, on-device speech-to-text, and the glue between them.
  • Product decisions in real time — why we cut features, why we kept the ugly-but-honest ones, and what we're betting on next.
  • Honest post-mortems — renames that almost broke things, animations we had to patch byte-by-byte, and everything else that never makes the landing page.

Where we are

The Windows app is live and improving weekly. The browser extension is shipped and tested. Mobile is on the roadmap. Under the hood we're investing in the boring stuff that makes speed possible: local-first data, on-device models where they beat the cloud, and a test suite that lets us move fast without leaving craters.

This blog is part of the product. If a post helped you, or you disagree with a call we made, tell us — the feedback loop is the point.

Next up: a story about renaming an entire product without breaking it, and how we performed surgery on a compiled animation file we couldn't rebuild. Come back — we'll be here.
